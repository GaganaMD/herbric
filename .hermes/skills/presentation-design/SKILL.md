# Presentation Design Skill

## Purpose

Generate presentation-quality slides with institutional visual standards.

Slides must feel:

- spacious
- analytical
- readable
- executive-grade
- information-dense without clutter

---

# Layout Philosophy

Slides should:

- maintain strong hierarchy
- balance visuals and text
- guide attention intentionally
- preserve readability in presentation mode

Avoid:

- overcrowding
- tiny text
- repetitive layouts
- weak alignment
- excessive bullet density

---

# Institutional Design Standard

Slides should resemble:

- investment banking materials
- PE diligence decks
- strategy consulting presentations
- institutional board materials

Avoid:

- generic AI aesthetics
- startup-template visuals
- decorative abstraction
- excessive gradients
- visual gimmicks

---

# Visual Standards

Use:

- strong visual anchors
- balanced spacing
- clean alignment
- readable typography
- high signal density
- institutional chart aesthetics

Avoid:

- filler visuals
- decorative AI graphics
- low-information imagery
- inconsistent spacing
- weak contrast

---
# Presentation-First Principle

This is fundamentally a visual presentation system.

Slides are not document pages exported into PowerPoint.

Every slide must:

* possess visual identity
* contain visual anchoring
* communicate through spatial hierarchy
* remain quickly understandable in presentation mode

At least one meaningful visual anchor should exist on nearly every slide unless analytically impossible.

Visual anchors may include:

* relevant operational/company imagery
* product visuals
* deployment visuals
* structured analytical charts
* comparison matrices
* KPI blocks
* process diagrams
* ecosystem visuals

Dense analytical text panels should be avoided.

The goal is:

* investor cognition
  not:
* maximum written explanation.

Presentation quality is equally important as analytical rigor.

Analytics should coexist with visual storytelling rather than replacing it.


---

# Chart Presentation

Charts must:

- remain readable in fullscreen mode
- communicate insights quickly
- use concise labels
- emphasize interpretation

Every chart must justify slide space.

---

# Slide Variety

Vary structures using:

- two-column layouts
- matrix layouts
- comparison grids
- KPI sections
- image-led slides
- timeline structures
- analytical callouts
- operating-model diagrams
- risk matrices

Avoid repetitive:
title + bullets layouts.

---

# Density Management

High information density is acceptable
ONLY if readability remains strong.

When overcrowded:

- split slides
- simplify visuals
- reduce text
- increase whitespace
- elevate hierarchy

---

# Slide Energy and Narrative Rhythm

Slides should feel distinct from one another.

Avoid decks where every slide becomes:

* text + chart + commentary
* visually repetitive
* tonally monotonous

The deck should alternate between:

* analytical slides
* strategic framing slides
* image-led slides
* synthesis slides
* risk framing slides
* market/context visuals

Narrative pacing matters.

A presentation should feel:

* dynamic
* visually progressive
* cognitively engaging

rather than:

* sequential analytical memo pages.

---

# Typography Rules

Typography must:

- remain readable from distance
- support hierarchy
- preserve visual calm
- avoid compression

Avoid:
tiny institutional footnote clutter.

---

# Final Standard

The deck should feel:

- institutionally credible
- presentation-ready
- visually coherent
- analytically sophisticated
- strategically polished

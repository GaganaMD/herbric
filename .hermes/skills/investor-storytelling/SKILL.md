# Investor Storytelling Skill

## Purpose

Convert research and analytics into institutional-quality investor narrative.

This skill determines:

- narrative flow
- strategic hierarchy
- slide sequencing
- conviction framing
- risk emphasis
- investment readability

---

# Narrative Philosophy

Presentations should resemble:

- PE screening decks
- investment committee material
- consulting strategy reviews
- institutional diligence reports

Avoid:

- startup hype
- marketing language
- shallow summaries
- generic SWOT content
- management optimism

---

# Anti-Fluff Principle

Avoid:

- strategic buzzword inflation
- decorative consulting language
- unsupported claims
- empty market positioning
- vague optimism

Prefer:

- evidence-backed interpretation
- operational specificity
- concise underwriting language
- measurable observations
- strategic clarity

---

# Conviction Framing

Slides should answer:

- what increases conviction?
- what weakens conviction?
- what evidence matters most?
- what could break the thesis?
- what supports scalability?
- what risks remain unresolved?

---

# Slide Purpose Doctrine

Every slide must answer one primary investor question.

Examples:

- Is growth durable?
- Is margin quality improving?
- Is customer concentration dangerous?
- Is the moat defensible?
- Is execution credibility increasing?
- Is the business operationally scalable?
- Is working capital becoming stressed?

If a slide does not answer a meaningful investor question,
it should be redesigned or removed.

---

# SWOT Expectations

SWOT analysis must:

- feel researched
- reflect operational reality
- reflect procurement realities
- reflect scaling constraints
- reflect execution fragility
- reflect competitive pressure
- reflect geopolitical constraints

Avoid MBA-style generic SWOT outputs.

---

# Interpretation Priority

Prioritize:

- insight density
- strategic meaning
- investor usefulness
- decision quality

Over:

- verbosity
- decorative writing
- filler commentary
- generic observations

---

# Narrative Structure

Narrative should progress logically:

1. Company grounding
2. Strategic relevance
3. Product/market positioning
4. Operational capability
5. Financial quality
6. Scalability assessment
7. Risk concentration
8. Diligence gaps
9. Conviction drivers
10. IC framing

---

# Final Standard

The presentation should feel like:

- institutional diligence material
- investment committee preparation
- underwriting support analysis

Not:
a startup pitch deck

### ONE SENTENCE SUMMARY:
Acme Robotics cut defects 18% with machine vision, but calibration failures, ignored alerts, and uneven maintenance staffing threaten reliable global scaling.

### MAIN POINTS:
1. Acme Robotics implemented machine-vision checkpoints in assembly operations.
2. Defect rates dropped by 18% following the checkpoint introduction.
3. One plant experienced rising quality incidents despite the broader improvement.
4. Sensor calibration drift contributed directly to that plant’s quality problems.
5. Alerts indicating calibration issues were ignored for three weeks.
6. Delayed response allowed quality issues to worsen before intervention.
7. Leadership states the machine-vision rollout can scale globally.
8. Global scalability depends on consistent maintenance execution.
9. Calibration staffing levels vary across different sites.
10. Inconsistent operational support creates risk to sustained quality gains.

### TAKEAWAYS:
1. Machine vision can significantly reduce defects when properly maintained.
2. Calibration discipline is essential to prevent localized quality regressions.
3. Ignored monitoring alerts can quickly negate technology-driven improvements.
4. Scalability claims require standardized staffing and maintenance readiness.
5. Operational consistency matters as much as deploying new quality technology.
